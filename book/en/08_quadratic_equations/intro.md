# Quadratic Equations

A quadratic equation is an equality of the form:

$$
ax^2 + bx + c = 0
$$

where:

- $a$, $b$ and $c$ are real numbers.
- $a \neq 0$.

## The discriminant

The discriminant is:

$$
\Delta = b^2 - 4ac
$$

It defines three cases:

- $\Delta > 0$ → two distinct real solutions.
- $\Delta = 0$ → one repeated real solution.
- $\Delta < 0$ → no real solutions.

## General formula

The solutions are given by:

$$
x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{\Delta}}{2a}
$$

### How to solve it

1. Identify the coefficients $a$, $b$ and $c$.
2. Compute the discriminant $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac$.
3. Check the sign of $\Delta$.
4. Substitute into the general formula.

## Worked example

Solve:

$$
2x^2 - 3x - 2 = 0
$$

Here $a = 2$, $b = -3$ and $c = -2$.

Compute the discriminant:

$$
\Delta = (-3)^2 - 4 \cdot 2 \cdot (-2) = 9 + 16 = 25
$$

Substitute into the formula:

$$
x = \frac{-(-3) \pm \sqrt{25}}{2 \cdot 2} = \frac{3 \pm 5}{4}
$$

The two solutions are:

- $x_1 = \frac{3 + 5}{4} = 2$
- $x_2 = \frac{3 - 5}{4} = -\frac{1}{2}$

## Interpretation

- A quadratic equation defines a parabola.
- If $\Delta > 0$, the parabola crosses the $x$-axis in two points.
- If $\Delta = 0$, the parabola touches the $x$-axis at one point.
- If $\Delta < 0$, the parabola does not cross the $x$-axis.

```{tip}
Verify the solutions by substituting them back into the original equation.
```
